# Trademark policy

The code is Apache-2.0. The **name** is not covered by that licence — Apache-2.0
grants copyright and patent rights and explicitly carves trademarks out. This is
the only exclusivity the project retains.

## You may, without asking

- Fork the project and distribute your fork under a **different** name.
- Say your product "works with Klopt", is "compatible with Klopt", or is "based
  on Klopt", as a factual statement.
- Use the name in documentation, articles, talks and comparisons.
- Run an instance for yourself or your clients.

## You may not, without written permission

- Call your fork or a modified distribution "Klopt", or a name likely to be
  confused with it.
- Use the name or logo in a way that implies official endorsement,
  certification, affiliation, or that your hosting is the official hosted
  offering.
- Register the name, a translation, or a confusingly similar mark as a
  trademark, domain, or company name.
- Use the logo as your product's logo.

## Why this exists

Under a permissive licence anyone may ship a modified copy. Users need one
reliable signal about what is the official build and who stands behind it, and
the name is that signal. Enforcement is about preventing confusion, never about
restricting the code.
